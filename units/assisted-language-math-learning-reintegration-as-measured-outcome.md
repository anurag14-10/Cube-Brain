## Unit

According to EkStep Foundation's AxL pathway, this is a strategic decision from the deployment.

**Decision.** Measure behavioural change — attendance, confidence, active classroom participation, willingness to attempt questions — as a primary outcome alongside diagnostic score improvement, rather than treating test performance as the sole success indicator.

**Alternative considered.** Measuring success purely through pre- and post-diagnostic score changes.

**Why.** The most significant early AML signal was behavioural reintegration: previously passive or absent students began attending more regularly, answering in class and expressing confidence — a change diagnostic scores undercount and the mechanism through which sustained learning improvement becomes possible.

**Context.** Students completing 1,000+ questions in a year — versus roughly 400–500 questions in a typical NCERT textbook across three years — provided the volume of successful repetition that built the confidence driving behavioural change.

## Condition

- **Applies when:** The target population includes students who have effectively disengaged from classroom learning; the programme provides sufficient successful repetitions at the right difficulty level to rebuild confidence before academic outcomes are visible in formal assessments.
- **Fails when:** Not documented in the source

## Before → after

- **Before:** Targeted students had been passive or absent in class, and pre- and post-diagnostic scores alone undercount the change from avoiding problems to voluntarily attempting them.
- **After:** A teacher survey of 617 respondents reported an average improvement of 5+ behavioural traits per student (including attendance, effort and time on task); respondents also flagged real infrastructure and connectivity problems.

## Why it is reusable

The condition tag says when this choice held in the source deployment; the alternative and the before→after show what a different choice would trade away. It is a suggestion from another adoption's lived experience, not a recommendation.
